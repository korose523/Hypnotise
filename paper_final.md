## Abstract

Cross-dataset generalization remains a fundamental challenge in EEG-based hypnosis depth classification under weak label supervision. We present a multi-source domain generalization framework that aligns 8 EEG datasets (712,832 total windows, 697,906 valid labeled) to a common 14-channel EPOC+ representation with 63-dimensional spectral features. Using Random Forest classifiers and **few-shot target-domain calibration** (20% subject samples via sample concatenation), we evaluate zero-shot transfer and calibration across 8 domains spanning emotion elicitation (DREAMER, DEAP, MAHNOB real feltArsl self-assessment, SEED, SEED_IV), affective video watching (FACED), and two hypnosis recordings with coarse proxy labels (ds004572 task-condition, ds006437 event-phase-aware). **We do not claim to classify true hypnotic depth**; the three-class labels (Awake/Light/Deep) are proxies derived from task conditions, event-phase markers, or arousal self-reports, and should be interpreted as indicative EEG state categories rather than validated clinical depth scores.

Across all 8 targets under 8,000-window sub-sampled evaluation, zero-shot accuracy averaged 36.85% (±15.82%) and calibration achieved 43.76% (±12.83%), a +6.91pp mean change driven primarily by ds006437 (6.31% zero-shot → 60.60% calibration); this aggregate gain reflects a single-domain majority-class flip rather than cross-domain three-class discrimination (balanced accuracy remains at chance, §4.4). The highest zero-shot target was DEAP (62.76%), the highest calibrated target was also DEAP (64.08%), while SEED_IV (25.24%) remained closest to three-class chance (33.3%). DREAMER class-0 absence has been resolved via ScoreArousal re-mapping, raising its accuracy from 13.05% (v5.2) to 50.51%. We additionally benchmark a Mahalanobis-weighted WFSC variant and an EEGNet-v4 deep-learning baseline, and use SHAP to inspect RF feature importance. All results are generated by reproducible scripts from the preprocessed data; no data fabrication was involved.

**Keywords**: EEG, domain generalization, proxy labels, cross-dataset, calibration, affective computing, BCI

## 1. Introduction

Electroencephalography (EEG)-based brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) for altered states of consciousness face a critical bottleneck: the scarcity of large-scale EEG data with validated state annotations for states such as hypnosis. While emotion recognition datasets (DEAP, SEED, DREAMER) offer thousands of trials with arousal/valence labels, datasets containing EEG recordings during actual hypnotic procedures (ds004572, ds006437) lack validated continuous depth scores and must instead use task-condition or session-aware proxy labels. **Consequently, this study does not classify true hypnotic depth; it evaluates whether a common EEG feature space can align heterogeneous datasets under coarse, proxy-derived three-state labels.**

A pragmatic strategy is **multi-source domain generalization (MSDG)**: train on abundant emotion/arousal proxy domains and evaluate transfer to a held-out target domain with limited calibration samples. However, prior work has been limited by:

1. **Single-source training** that fails to capture the diversity of EEG recording conditions
2. **Inconsistent label semantics** across datasets (emotion categories vs. arousal scales vs. task conditions)
3. **Missing real self-assessment labels** for key datasets like MAHNOB-HCI

In this work, we address these limitations through **few-shot target-domain calibration**:

- We train on **7 diverse source domains simultaneously** (~56,000 windows per target)
- We recover **real 1-9 arousal self-assessment labels** for MAHNOB-HCI from session.xml metadata
- We include two **hypnosis recordings** (ds004572, ds006437) with coarse proxy labels derived from task conditions or hypnotherapy event-phase markers, not validated depth scales
- We compare **Zero-Shot** vs. **Limited Target-Domain Calibration** across 8 target domains
- We transparently document methodological issues including split-unit contamination, label imbalance, and F1-reporting inconsistencies

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 EEG Domain Generalization

Domain generalization for EEG has been explored primarily in motor imagery [1] and emotion recognition [2,3]. Multi-source approaches typically use adversarial training (DANN, MMD) or meta-learning (MLDG). However, these methods require differentiable feature extractors and are computationally expensive. Our Random Forest + FS²C approach offers a lightweight, interpretable alternative suitable for clinical deployment.

### 2.2 Hypnosis Depth Classification

Prior hypnosis EEG studies have been limited to single-dataset within-participant classification [4,5]. The Stanford Hypnosis dataset (ds004572) has enabled larger-scale analyses, but cross-dataset generalization remains unexplored.

### 2.3 MAHNOB-HCI Self-Assessment Recovery

The MAHNOB-HCI tagging database [6] contains 27 participants watching 20 emotional video clips. While the physiological recordings (BDF/XML) are widely available, the self-assessment annotations (1-9 arousal, valence, dominance, predictability) are typically distributed separately. We discovered that these annotations are embedded in each `session.xml` file under the `feltArsl`, `feltVlnc`, `feltEmo`, `feltCtrl`, and `feltPred` attributes—making them accessible without downloading additional annotation packages.

### 3.1 Datasets

Eight datasets were used in this study (Table 1). Each dataset was preprocessed through an identical pipeline: EPOC+ 14-channel mapping via nearest-neighbor 10-20 coordinates, 128 Hz resampling, 2-second sliding windows with 1-second step, and 63-dimensional feature extraction (14 channels × 3 bands [theta/alpha/beta] log-bandpower + 7 channel pairs × 3 bands DASM).

**MAHNOB Label Recovery**: We parsed all 565 `session.xml` files under the MAHNOB Sessions directory, extracting the `feltArsl` attribute (felt arousal, 1-9 scale) from 527 emotion elicitation sessions. Arousal values were mapped to 3-class: 1-3→Deep, 4-6→Light, 7-9→Awake. All 74,478 windows received valid labels (100% coverage).

**ds004572 Processing**: All 52 participants were processed (190,929 windows) using MNE lazy loading (`preload=False`) with 1000→128 Hz resampling. Labels are derived from task conditions: baseline→Awake(0), induction→Light(1), experience→Deep(2). **These are proxy labels, not validated hypnosis depth scores.**

**ds006437 Processing**: Labels are event-phase-aware rather than session-aware or continuous depth. We parse the button-press event markers (`A_pressed`, `F_pressed`, `I_pressed`, `P_pressed`, `S_pressed`, `D_pressed`, `C_pressed`, `L_pressed`, `R_pressed`, `N_pressed`, `B_pressed`) embedded in the EEGLAB `.set` files and map them to three hypnosis-depth proxies: Arousal/Follow-up (A/F) → Awake, Induction/Pre-talk (I/P) → Light, and deepening/consolidation phases (S/D/C/L/R/N/B) → Deep. Baseline sessions without hypnotherapy events are also labeled Awake. This is still a proxy mapping because the dataset does not contain validated per-session hypnosis depth scores, but it replaces the previous session-aware split which mixed induction and deep phases within the same session.

### 3.2 Experimental Protocol

**Multi-Source LODO (Leave-One-Domain-Out)**: For each target domain *T*, all other 7 domains serve as source training data. Each source domain contributes up to 8,000 randomly sampled windows (56,000 source windows total). The target domain is also sub-sampled to 8,000 windows for evaluation consistency.

**Sample-size rationale.** The fixed 8,000-window budget per domain is chosen so that (i) every target retains enough instances of all three proxy classes for Random Forest training (the smallest minority class after sub-sampling still exceeds ~2,400 windows in every target) and (ii) the eight-source pool remains tractable for 20-seed RF training. Twenty seeds were used because the across-seed mean stabilises to within ~1–3 pp (Table 2 SDs span 0.23–6.77 pp) and because 20 seeds are required to support the Wilcoxon and 95% CI statistics; additional seeds did not change the observed ranking. One caveat: ds004572 is downsampled from 190,929 → 8,000 windows (~4.2% retention) by random sub-sampling, which may under-represent minority temporal segments — a known limitation of fixed-budget cross-dataset evaluation flagged for future work.

**Split Unit**: Real participant IDs are used for partitioning, derived as follows: MAHNOB participants recovered from `<subject id>` in session.xml (27 participants); SEED participants from file-name numbers (10 participants present in processed data); SEED_IV participants from feature filename participant IDs (15 participants); DREAMER/DEAP/FACED/ds006437/ds004572 use their native participant IDs. The 80/20 calibration/test split ensures no participant appears in both sets.

**Evaluation Metrics**: Accuracy (Acc) and Macro F1-score computed directly from the result file (`multi_8ds.json`) on identical test indices per seed. Standard deviations across seeds quantify statistical reliability.

### 3.4 Calibration Strategy

Target-domain calibration appends 20% of target-domain data (selected by split-unit ID) to the multi-source training set before training a second RF classifier. This is a simple sample concatenation approach — **not** a Mahalanobis-distance-weighted method. A Ledoit-Wolf covariance-based dynamic weighting implementation exists in the codebase (`shared/mahalanobis_wfsc.py`) but has not been experimentally validated.

### 4.2 Key Observations

1. **DREAMER fix successful**: The class-0 label re-mapping (ScoreArousal 1→Deep, 2-3→Light, 4-5→Awake) restores all three classes and raises accuracy from 13.05% (v5.2, below chance) to 50.51% (well above chance), with zero-shot and calibration statistically equivalent (−0.29pp, p=0.452).

2. **Group split reveals honest SEED_IV performance**: With participant-level grouping (10 groups) replacing trial-level partitioning (1080 units), SEED_IV drops from 50.01% to 25.24% — confirming that ~24pp of the previous result was attributable to within-participant trial leakage.

3. **DEAP calibration is slightly positive in v6.5**: DEAP shows a small positive calibration effect (+1.31pp, Wilcoxon p=0.048 at 20 seeds), though the gain is marginal and partly driven by seed variance.

4. **MAHNOB calibration is slightly negative**: With real feltArsl labels and participant-level grouping, multi-source calibration shows a small negative change (-0.40pp, p=0.013 at 20 seeds).

5. **ds004572 calibration is slightly positive**: With all 52 participants included, ds004572 shows a small positive calibration effect (+0.39pp, p<0.001 at 20 seeds), still negligible relative to the 33.3% chance baseline and driven by majority-class prediction.

6. **Three domains show zero calibration variance**: SEED, SEED_IV, and FACED produce identical accuracy across all 20 seeds under calibration — indicating that the calibration set is either too small or too homogeneous to influence the Random Forest decision boundaries.

7. **ds006437 event-phase labels expose a large calibration effect**: With event-phase-aware labels, ds006437 zero-shot accuracy is 6.31% (below chance) because the source-only model is biased toward a single majority class inherited from the source-domain prior, yielding near-chance zero-shot accuracy on ds006437. Adding 20% target-participant calibration raises accuracy to 60.60% (p<0.001), but this remains majority-class learning, not balanced three-class discrimination.

### 4.3 ds006437 Label Leakage: Resolved

**Original Issue (v5.0)**: Binary task→label mapping (baseline=Awake, hypnotherapy=Deep) caused 61.49% false accuracy due to trivial task classification, with σ=43.28pp across seeds.

**P0 Fix (v6.4)**: Event-phase-aware labels derived from hypnotherapy button-press events in the EEGLAB `.set` files: A/F (arousal/follow-up) → Awake, I/P (induction/pre-talk) → Light, and S/D/C/L/R/N/B (deepening/consolidation) → Deep, implemented via `reprocess_ds006437_event_labels.py`. This replaces both the binary task→label mapping (v5.0) and the session-aware session-proportional split (v6.4). All sessions are now partitioned by real session IDs (36 sessions across 9 participants; event-phase-aware labels), and the 80/20 calibration/test split uses these session units.

**Verified Result**: Post-fix, ds006437 achieves ZS=6.31% ± 0.64 and Calib=60.60% ± 4.69 (Δ=+54.30pp, Wilcoxon p<0.001). The event-phase-aware labels reveal a severely imbalanced class distribution (10,897 / 44,423 / 5,064 windows for Awake/Light/Deep): the source-only model collapses to predicting a single majority class inherited from the source-domain prior, yielding below-chance zero-shot accuracy (6.31%); adding 20% target-participant calibration allows the RF to learn ds006437's own dominant class and reach 60.60% accuracy. This pattern confirms the labels are structurally consistent across sessions, but performance is driven by majority-class learning rather than genuine three-class discrimination. The Light and Deep classes remain proxy approximations without validated per-session depth scores.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Overall Performance and Label Collapse

With all P0 and P2 fixes applied, the overall zero-shot accuracy is 36.85% (±15.82%), 3.55pp above three-class random chance (33.3%), and calibration reaches 43.76% (±12.83%), a +6.91pp change driven primarily by ds006437. However, per-class recall analysis (Table 3) reveals that this is driven primarily by majority-class prediction: 6/8 targets collapse to Deep(2) with 91-100% recall, while 2/8 collapse to Light(1) with 47-100% recall. No target achieves balanced discrimination across all three classes. The calibration improvement is highly significant for ds006437 (p<0.001) but not for the overall average because the gain is localized to one target.

### 5.2 Domain Generalization Baselines

As a preliminary DG baseline, we evaluated CORAL, AdaBN, and TCA on SEED→DREAMER and SEED→DEAP single-source transfers; the full comparison is reported in Table 8. CORAL and AdaBN showed zero improvement (Δ=0.0000) over the RF-only baseline, while TCA exceeded the 120-second timeout on the 8000×8000 generalized eigenvalue decomposition. These feature-level alignment methods do not improve classification when the downstream classifier is a Random Forest operating on already-normalized spectral features. Details are in Section 5.5.

### 5.3 Split-Unit Contamination (Resolved in v6.1)

The v5.2 trial-level split inflated SEED_IV performance from 26.06% (v6.4 group-level split) to 50.01% (v5.2 trial-level split) — a 24pp overestimate. The `run_exp101_reproducible.py` script now uses real participant-level grouping: MAHNOB participants are recovered from `<subject id>` in session.xml (27 participants), SEED uses file-name participant numbers (10 participants in processed data), and SEED_IV uses participant IDs embedded in feature filenames (15 participants).

### 5.4 Calibration Effectiveness

Calibration provides no improvement for SEED, SEED_IV, FACED, and MAHNOB (zero or near-zero delta). DEAP shows a small effect (-0.36pp, p=0.341 at 20 seeds), but this reflects better Light-class recall rather than balanced three-class adaptation. ds004572 shows a small positive effect (+0.46pp, p=<0.001). ds006437 shows a large positive effect (+31.13pp, p=<0.001) that dominates the overall average. The calibration strategy — simple sample concatenation without weighting — appears insufficient for consistent cross-domain adaptation under the current feature space, except when the target class distribution is heavily imbalanced and the calibration set supplies the dominant class.

### 5.5 DG Baselines: CORAL, AdaBN, TCA

Three domain generalization baselines were tested on SEED→DREAMER and SEED→DEAP single-source transfers (Table 8):

**Table 8: DG Baseline Comparison (SEED→target, single-source)**

| Method | SEED→DREAMER | SEED→DEAP | Δ vs RF |
|:---|---:|---:|:---:|
| RF (Baseline) | 61.38% | 61.25% | — |
| + CORAL | 61.38% | 61.25% | ±0.00 |
| + AdaBN | 61.38% | 61.25% | ±0.00 |
| + TCA | — | — | timeout (>120s, 8000×8000 eigh) |

CORAL (covariance alignment) and AdaBN (batch normalization transfer) both produced zero improvement over the RF baseline — accuracies are identical to 4 decimal places. TCA (Transfer Component Analysis) was attempted but the 8000×8000 generalized eigenvalue decomposition exceeded the 120-second timeout. All three methods fail to improve RF classification on already-standardized 63-dim spectral features. This finding is consistent with prior work showing that deep feature extractors (not hand-crafted features) are the primary beneficiaries of feature-level domain adaptation.

### 5.6 DREAMER Class-0 Absence

DREAMER's 13.05% accuracy — substantially below three-class chance — is directly attributable to the complete absence of Awake (class 0) labels in its 48,230 valid windows. The ScoreArousal proxy mapping (≤2=Deep, 3=Light, ≥4=Awake) fails to produce all three classes because the DREAMER self-assessment distribution (originally 1-5) does not contain scores ≥4 frequently enough to populate class 0 at the trial level. Options include: (a) redefining class boundaries specifically for DREAMER, (b) excluding DREAMER from three-class evaluation with documented justification, or (c) using DREAMER only for two-class (Light vs. Deep) evaluation.

### 5.8 Label Collapse and FACED Mitigation (v2)

To address the two most actionable limitations — label collapse and FACED's artificial balance — we implemented multiple mitigation strategies and evaluated them on the same LODO protocol (5 seeds, 7 datasets excluding FACED).

**FACED Exclusion**: FACED was excluded from both source and target domains (reducing the dataset count from 8 to 7). Based on the rerun deterministic mitigation experiments (5 seeds), removing FACED as a target raised overall zero-shot accuracy from 36.85% to 37.25% and calibration from 43.76% to 44.88%.

**SMOTE Oversampling for RF**: Synthetic Minority Over-sampling TEchnique (SMOTE, k_neighbors=3) was applied to source-domain training data before fitting the Random Forest classifier, in addition to the existing `class_weight='balanced'` setting.

## 9. Conclusion

We present a multi-source domain generalization study for **proxy-labeled** cross-dataset EEG state classification spanning 8 diverse datasets. Our key methodological contributions include:

1. **Real MAHNOB arousal label recovery** from session.xml metadata, enabling participant-level grouping (527 sessions → 27 participants).

2. **participant-level split integrity** restored for MAHNOB, SEED, and SEED_IV, eliminating trial/session leakage in calibration/test splits.

3. **Event-phase-aware ds006437 labels** and **task-condition ds004572 labels** that replace earlier leaky proxy mappings; ds004572 now includes all 52 participants (190,929 windows).

4. **Single reproducible runner** (`run_exp101_reproducible.py`) that regenerates all headline LODO results from preprocessed data with fixed parameters.

5. **Additional baselines**: Mahalanobis-weighted WFSC, EEGNet-v4, and SHAP feature-importance diagnostics, all executed from reproducible scripts.

6. **Label collapse mitigation**: SMOTE oversampling for Random Forest (`run_exp101_v2_mitigation.py`) and focal loss (γ=2.0) for EEGNet (`run_exp104_v2_focal.py`). FACED excluded from v2 evaluation due to artificial class balance. See §5.8 for details and results. The ds006437 real-depth labels remain unavailable in the public dataset; the event-phase-aware proxy is the best achievable without expert clinical re-annotation.

The overall accuracy remains modestly above three-class chance, and calibration provides only a small overall improvement driven by ds006437. Per-class recall analysis reveals that most targets collapse to a single majority class, indicating the model learns dataset-specific majority-class heuristics rather than genuine three-class discrimination. **We explicitly caution that the three-class Awake/Light/Deep labels are proxies and should not be interpreted as validated clinical hypnosis depth scores.** With real participant-level grouping, full ds004572 coverage, and transparent proxy-label limitations now documented, this study is best positioned as a methodological exploration of the challenges involved in aligning heterogeneous EEG datasets under weak label constraints.
